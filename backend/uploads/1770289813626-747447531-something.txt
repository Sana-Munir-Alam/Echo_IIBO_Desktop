The system didn’t fail outright — it hesitated.

During transmission, the analyzer flagged a delayed fragment, not a full word.
Only two characters surfaced before the stream resumed:

C 	O

They weren’t corrupted — just late.

The delay wasn’t random either.
Logs show a latency of 10 units, meaning the fragment arrived TEN steps behind where it should have appeared in the sequence.

Engineers noted that delayed fragments don’t disappear — they simply arrive out of place.
To reconstruct the original message, the fragment must be shifted back by the amount of latency and re-inserted where it belongs.
